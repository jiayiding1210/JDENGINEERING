NDT & FIELD INVESTIGATION

Measuring condition without disrupting service

Nondestructive evaluation and field investigation provide information that visual inspection or drawings alone cannot supply. The goal is to characterize condition, geometry, material response, defects, and in-service behavior with the least practical disruption to the structure.

MEASUREMENT FRAMEWORK

From observation to quantified structural information

No single NDE method answers every question. Effective investigation begins by defining the suspected deterioration mechanism or structural uncertainty, then combining complementary technologies with targeted verification and engineering interpretation.

01

Vision-based inspection

High-resolution photography, photogrammetry, UAS, and 3D reconstruction can document inaccessible surfaces, map cracks and spalls, establish repeatable visual records, and support geometry capture.

02

Concrete NDE

Ground-penetrating radar, impact echo, ultrasonic methods, infrared thermography, electrical measurements, and related techniques can help identify delamination, voids, deterioration, moisture, corrosion risk, and internal anomalies.

03

Steel & connection assessment

Ultrasonic testing, magnetic particle inspection, dye penetrant methods, radiography, and targeted measurements can support fatigue-crack detection, weld evaluation, section-loss assessment, and connection investigation.

04

Optical full-field measurement

Digital image correlation and other non-contact optical techniques can measure displacement and strain fields without attaching conventional sensors directly to the structure, supporting laboratory and field validation.

05

Structural health monitoring

Strain, displacement, acceleration, temperature, corrosion, acoustic, fiber-optic, and other sensors can track structural response over time when a clear decision framework exists for interpreting the data.

06

Diagnostic load testing

Controlled loading paired with instrumentation can quantify load distribution, stiffness, boundary behavior, continuity, and model bias, providing evidence for model calibration and evaluation decisions.

FIELD CONSIDERATIONS

Technology must support the engineering question

  • Define the target defect or response. The sensing physics must be appropriate for the material, depth, orientation, and type of deterioration being investigated.
  • Use complementary methods. Combining visual, NDE, physical verification, and structural response measurements reduces ambiguity.
  • Understand resolution and uncertainty. Detection limits, calibration, environmental noise, access geometry, surface condition, and operator skill can strongly affect results.
  • Do not replace tactile inspection blindly. Remote and automated tools can improve coverage and safety, but some conditions still require physical access, sounding, probing, or direct verification.
  • Convert data into decisions. Large point clouds, images, waveforms, or sensor streams are valuable only when translated into condition maps, model parameters, thresholds, and actionable engineering conclusions.

CURRENT DIRECTION

AI and vision systems are expanding inspection capability

Recent development is increasingly focused on automated crack mapping, machine-learning interpretation of NDE datasets, UAS-enabled imaging, mixed-reality inspection support, fiber-optic monitoring, and persistent digital records. These tools can increase coverage and consistency, but qualified engineering review, validation, and appropriate physical confirmation remain essential.
