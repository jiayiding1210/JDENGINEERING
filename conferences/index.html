Presentation 01

Design of Complex Bridge Substructure Using the Strut-and-Tie Method

This presentation presented a comprehensive case study on the design of complex substructures for a mile-long viaduct bridge in Orlando, Florida, with emphasis on two common pier-cap configurations: inverted-T caps and hammerhead caps. Both configurations are supported by a single column located near the center of the cap, while the inverted-T arrangement was used to address vertical-clearance constraints where the viaduct crosses an existing roadway.

The presentation examined the load-transfer mechanisms from superstructure bearings through these deep-beam regions and demonstrated why classic beam theory is not sufficient to represent their behavior. The strut-and-tie method, or truss analogy, was used to model the primary force paths and was compared with finite element analysis using solid elements for concrete and truss elements for reinforcement and post-tensioning. The comparison highlighted differences in predicted stress distributions and deformations, as well as the strengths and limitations of each analytical approach.

Topology optimization was also used to explore efficient load paths within the substructure. The case study connected analytical theory, finite element modeling, detailing, and engineering judgment to develop practical recommendations for complex bridge substructure design.

Presentation 02

Soil–Structure Interaction Analysis of Balanced Cantilever Segmental Bridges

This presentation focused on the analysis of a complex four-span continuous post-tensioned cast-in-place segmental bridge in the mountainous region of Colorado. The project involved challenging terrain, variable soil conditions, and the need for refined representation of substructure and foundation stiffness.

The work incorporated soil–structure interaction and cracked-section analysis to improve representation of bridge behavior under static and dynamic loading. A multi-software approach was used to leverage the strengths of different analysis platforms, allowing specialized components of the design to be modeled efficiently and then connected through an integrated workflow. The analysis also considered construction staging and time-dependent effects including creep and shrinkage.

The presentation explained the process used to determine effective substructure stiffness through a moment–curvature approach and described the cracked-section analysis procedure in detail. The case study illustrated how advanced analysis tools, engineering judgment, and coordinated workflows can be combined to address complex bridge design problems while maintaining a reliable and transparent understanding of structural behavior.

PRESENTATION

Connecting digital tools with practical bridge engineering

FAISE 2026

Digital Transformation of Structural Engineering in Design, Inspection, and Asset Management

The forum brought together a highly engaged and diverse group from across the transportation and infrastructure community. Approximately 70 participants attended, including representatives from FDOT Districts 4 and 6, South Florida counties, engineering consultants, contractors, suppliers, and academic partners.

Jiayi presented how digital tools can strengthen bridge structural design and project delivery. Examples included parametric design using platforms such as OpenBridge Modeler for complex interchange projects, improving coordination between structural, roadway, and other disciplines while making design development more efficient and consistent.

The presentation also highlighted digital image correlation as a non-contact measurement technology for structural health monitoring. By capturing deformation and displacement without conventional contact sensors, digital image correlation can support efficient and economical field evaluation while expanding the role of digital data in inspection and asset-management decisions.

PRESENTATIONS

Advanced analysis, integrated modeling, and practical bridge design

Presentation 01

Analysis and Design of a Complex Post-Tensioned Segmental Bridge

This presentation examined the analysis and independent design check of a complex four-span post-tensioned segmental bridge in Colorado, where mountainous terrain, construction staging, variable foundation conditions, and soil–structure interaction required a refined analytical approach.

The work integrated longitudinal and transverse superstructure analysis, substructure stiffness evaluation, foundation design, and soil–structure interaction using specialized software platforms. Construction-stage effects, post-tensioning, time-dependent behavior, moving loads, temperature, wind, and foundation stiffness were incorporated into the design-review process, with iterative feedback between the global bridge model and foundation analysis.

The presentation highlighted how coordinated multi-software workflows can improve model fidelity and help engineers understand the interaction between superstructure, substructure, and foundations while maintaining clear and reviewable design checks.

Presentation team: Porter Hawkins, Jiayi Ding, Quan Do, and Daniel Castillo.

Presentation 02

Design of Bridge Inverted-T and Hammerhead Pier Based on Strut-and-Tie Method

This case study focused on two deep pier-cap configurations for the CFX SR 414 Expressway Extension: inverted-T and hammerhead piers. The presentation followed the load path from superstructure bearings through the disturbed regions of the pier caps and demonstrated how the strut-and-tie method can be used to develop practical reinforcement layouts for complex concrete substructures.

The presentation compared strut-and-tie behavior with finite element analysis, emphasizing the importance of identifying realistic force paths, selecting governing load cases, checking nodes and tie anchorage, and providing adequate crack-control reinforcement. The work also showed how refined analysis can be used to validate and optimize a conservative STM-based design approach.

Presentation team: David Konz, Jiayi Ding, Kishan Singh, and Herman Rucker.

TopicRehabilitation and validation of precast prestressed slab beam bridges

PRESENTATION

Rehabilitation and field validation for precast prestressed slab beam bridges.

TIP Day 2024

Rehabilitation Method, Experimental Work, and Diagnostic Load Testing

This presentation shared a rehabilitation approach developed for precast prestressed slab beam bridges, where deterioration of the shear-key connection can become a recurring maintenance issue and lead to loss of effective transverse load distribution between adjacent beams.

The work focused on restoring more monolithic bridge behavior while minimizing traffic impacts during construction. The rehabilitation concept was evaluated through experimental work and diagnostic load testing to assess its effectiveness and to compare structural behavior before and after rehabilitation.

The presentation emphasized the value of combining analytical understanding, laboratory validation, and field measurements to develop a practical alternative to full bridge replacement. For owners managing aging precast bridge inventories, this type of targeted rehabilitation can provide a more efficient option when replacement is not yet necessary or would create substantial cost and traffic disruption.

Bridge Foundation Design

DateJuly 12, 2024
ConferenceASCE Florida Section Annual Conference
ThemeResiliency & Sustainability
TopicBridge Foundation Design

PRESENTATION & SITE VISIT

Foundation engineering for resilient, durable bridge infrastructure

Bridge Foundation Design

Jiayi presented a practical overview of bridge foundation engineering covering structural, geotechnical, and construction considerations. The presentation addressed geotechnical investigation, foundation-type selection, analysis and design, pile testing, and project-specific considerations that influence an efficient and economical foundation solution.

Consistent with the conference theme of resiliency and sustainability, the presentation emphasized designing foundations for demanding service conditions, including scour, ship impact, and seismic effects, while considering durability, constructability, long-term operation, and service-life performance. The discussion also highlighted emerging foundation technologies and research-based approaches that can improve constructability and infrastructure resilience.

The conference also included a site visit to the post-Hurricane Ian Sanibel Causeway restoration project, providing a direct look at reconstruction work in a coastal environment where resilience, durability, and rapid restoration of critical transportation infrastructure are central engineering considerations.

Presentation team: Jiayi Ding, Seyed Saman Khedmatgozar Dolati, and Armin Mehrabi.
