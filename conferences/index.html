PRESENTATIONS

Presentation 01

Design of Complex Bridge Substructure Using the Strut-and-Tie Method

This presentation presented a comprehensive case study on the design of complex substructures for a mile-long viaduct bridge in Orlando, Florida, with emphasis on two common pier-cap configurations: inverted-T caps and hammerhead caps. Both configurations are supported by a single column located near the center of the cap, while the inverted-T arrangement was used to address vertical-clearance constraints where the viaduct crosses an existing roadway.

The presentation examined the load-transfer mechanisms from superstructure bearings through these deep-beam regions and demonstrated why classic beam theory is not sufficient to represent their behavior. The strut-and-tie method, or truss analogy, was used to model the primary force paths and was compared with finite element analysis using solid elements for concrete and truss elements for reinforcement and post-tensioning. The comparison highlighted differences in predicted stress distributions and deformations, as well as the strengths and limitations of each analytical approach.

Topology optimization was also used to explore efficient load paths within the substructure. The case study connected analytical theory, finite element modeling, detailing, and engineering judgment to develop practical recommendations for complex bridge substructure design.

Presentation 02

Soil–Structure Interaction Analysis of Balanced Cantilever Segmental Bridges

This presentation focused on the analysis of a complex four-span continuous post-tensioned cast-in-place segmental bridge in the mountainous region of Colorado. The project involved challenging terrain, variable soil conditions, and the need for refined representation of substructure and foundation stiffness.

The work incorporated soil–structure interaction and cracked-section analysis to improve representation of bridge behavior under static and dynamic loading. A multi-software approach was used to leverage the strengths of different analysis platforms, allowing specialized components of the design to be modeled efficiently and then connected through an integrated workflow. The analysis also considered construction staging and time-dependent effects including creep and shrinkage.

The presentation explained the process used to determine effective substructure stiffness through a moment–curvature approach and described the cracked-section analysis procedure in detail. The case study illustrated how advanced analysis tools, engineering judgment, and coordinated workflows can be combined to address complex bridge design problems while maintaining a reliable and transparent understanding of structural behavior.
